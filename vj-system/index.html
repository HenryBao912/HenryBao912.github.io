VJ System

Unity · C# · For a media company, Beijing, 2025

A real-time audio visualizer and VJ tool built in Unity for a brand IP launch, driving generative visuals from live audio input. I built it for an 8K screen shaped like an upside-down L, the largest display in Beijing, which with smaller screens around it formed a 360-degree digital experience. It is made to be played live: at events and festivals an operator moves the point of view and creates custom visuals on the spot.

Everything on screen is driven by one signal. Every frame the tool reads the sound as 512 frequency bins and folds them into eight bands. The bins raise a ring of 512 bars, the bands drive eight columns and the letters of the logo, and overall loudness lifts the bar above it. The input is a live microphone or a song file, switched from an on-screen menu.

Three fixed cameras. The ring is the spectrum, one bar per frequency bin, with the bass at the right where the tall spikes are. Low inside the ring with a long lens, the same bars stand up as a bar graph; from overhead the ring is a circle and the logo a line across it. The eight magenta columns are the bands, each letter of the logo glows with one band, and the blue bar rises with overall loudness.

Three camera moves: in from the side through the bass spikes, a spiral down from overhead, and a long-lens pan along the bars. At events the operator flies the camera live on keyboard and mouse to change the point of view; for these recordings the moves were scripted so every take runs the same way.*

*For these recordings the client’s logo was replaced with a placeholder word, and the visualizer listened to a song file, “Alone” by BoDleasons from Pixabay, instead of a live microphone. Open a clip full screen and unmute it to hear the track it is reacting to.

How it’s built

Eight bands from 512 bins
Each frame the tool takes a 512-bin spectrum of the sound through a Blackman window and folds it into eight bands that double in width, 2 bins, then 4, 8 and on up to 256, so each band covers about an octave. Bins are weighted by their position so the treble bands still move next to the bass.
Snap up, ease down
A band that jumps is followed at once, but a band that drops falls through a buffer whose speed starts small and grows by 5% every frame. The columns and letters hit on the beat and settle smoothly instead of flickering at frame rate.
Levels that set themselves
Loudness is measured against the loudest moment the tool has heard so far, so the blue bar uses its full range whether the input is a quiet microphone in the room or a mastered track.
The logo as an instrument
Each letter gets its own material, and its glow follows one band through a 2.2 power curve, so quiet passages stay dark and hits flare. The word lights up from the bass on the left to the treble on the right.
Built to be played live
The operator flies a free camera on keyboard and mouse to change the point of view mid-set, and Esc opens a menu that switches between the microphone and a song file. Both inputs feed the same audio source, so everything downstream is identical, and the microphone records into a looping ten-second buffer, so it can run for as long as the event does.
1,023 bars in one draw call
A later pass rebuilt the ring with GPU instancing: instead of 512 GameObjects, a single Graphics.DrawMeshInstanced call draws 1,023 bars from matrices rebuilt from the spectrum each frame. The clips show the original 512-bar ring.
